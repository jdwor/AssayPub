# Design Context

Derived from the founding brief (November 2026). Edit freely; design work in this repo reads this file first.

## Target audience

Working scientists, AI-for-science researchers and builders, funders and science-policy people. Expert readers who are short on time and suspicious of hype. A second audience is machine: research agents that read the structured feeds.

## Use cases

- Read this month's edition in ten minutes and know what mattered in AI for science, and how far to trust it.
- Open one work and see what it claims, what supports the claim, what was checked, and what is in doubt.
- Browse past editions and follow a topic as evidence accumulates.
- Cite a selection or assessment by a stable address.

## Brand personality and tone

Rigorous, plain, exacting. A printed bulletin more than a magazine: it states what was released, how far to trust it, and where to read it. The publication is called Assay (working title): to assay is to test ore for what is valuable.

## Aesthetic direction

Chosen by the editor on November 17, 2026 from eight mockups (`mockups/`, direction A3 "Matrix"). The first design, with warm paper, a serif text face and generated illustrations, was rejected as looking machine-made; it is kept in `archive/design-1/`.

- **One idea:** the cover is the table of contents. It prints the plate: eight rows, one per topic, ten wells each (a setting; twelve gives the standard 96-well plate), with the row's selected works set beside it. To the right of the plate a panel carries the edition's own pages: the month in small capitals, the editorial's subject as a heading (one word where it can be), its first paragraph, a button to the rest, and the Shortlist and any Follow-ups with their counts. A ring is an empty well, a ring with a dot is a shortlisted work, a filled well is a selected work, and a double ring is a lead selection. Works are addressed by edition and well, for example "Assay 0 · F7".
- **Color:** one flat cover color per edition (`cover` in the edition file; the build rotates through a palette when it is not set), black ink, white inside pages. The cover color returns inside only as the top strip, link hover and the "disputed" chip. No gradients, tints, shadows or rounded cards.
- **Type:** Besley (slab, weights to 900) for the wordmark, titles and cover entries; Archivo, condensed to 64–78% width, for well codes, big row letters and capital labels; Literata for running text. All self-hosted in `site/static/fonts/`.
- **Inside pages:** a sticky cover-colored strip, then two columns: a narrow column that opens with one oversized condensed token (the well code, the edition number, the topic letter) over a ruled list of facts, and a 43rem text column. Sections are separated by 4px black rules.
- **Two levels of navigation, kept apart:** Editorial and Shortlist belong to an edition and live in the cover's panel and each inside page's side column. Editions, Topics, Method and Charter belong to the publication and live in the cover's bottom line and the strip. The bottom line also links to the previous and next edition.
- **The editorial.** Each edition's editorial argues something about the period from its works; it is not a list of items. Its first paragraph is printed on the cover under its subject. Follow-ups, when an edition has any, are a short list at the foot of the editorial page. The page carries no status sentence, no account of how the edition was made and no list of what was not covered; that belongs on the Method page or in the editor's report.
- **Words on the page:** labels only. No explanatory captions under headings, no taglines beyond the one on the cover, no instructions for how to read the plate.
- **Motion:** the filled wells scale in once when the cover loads. Hovering or focusing a well shows a black label beside it with its address, status, title and source, and highlights its entry in the row; hovering an entry highlights its well. The label is fixed to the viewport, and nothing on the cover changes size on hover. Reduced-motion is respected.
- **Masthead:** the name, the tagline and the edition number stand on one baseline. The month is not in the masthead; it heads the panel, above the editorial's subject. The tagline stays in the small condensed capitals of the labels; on November 17, 2026 the editor tried a slab italic and a heavier, larger setting and rejected both. An unsigned edition carries no status line; a signed one says who signed it. On a narrow screen the tagline drops to its own line under the name.
- **Archive:** each edition is its plate, the month and the editorial's subject. No counts.
- **Rows of the plate:** on a wide screen the eight rows are bands of equal height, and the row letter, the wells and the row's titles are each centered on the band's axis, so the wells form a regular grid and every rule falls midway between two rows. A band grows only when its titles need more than an equal share. (Before November 17, 2026 the rows were as tall as their titles and the spare height went between them, which left each row sitting on the rule beneath it.)
- **Phones (to 44rem).** There is no room to set titles beside wells, so the plate is printed whole under the masthead, eight rows in one block, and stays the first thing on the screen. Below it, in order: the editorial (month, subject, first paragraph, button), each row's titles as a ruled list without wells (well codes in their own column, one title to a line), the Shortlist with its count, and the bottom line. A row letter on the plate jumps to its row in the list. Before November 18, 2026 the phone cover opened on the editorial and broke the plate into eight strips with titles between them; the editor found that wanting.
- **Touch.** A finger covers a label set beside a well, so on touch the label is a black bar along the bottom of the window: address and status in the cover color, the title (two lines at most), the source, and an arrow. The first touch of a well shows the bar and marks the well; the bar, or a second touch of the same well, opens the work. Touching anything else, or scrolling, puts it away. The touch area is the whole cell, whatever the size of the well drawn in it.
- **Tablets and narrow windows (44 to 64rem).** One column: the plate first, with titles still beside the wells and the rows as equal bands, then the panel as a band beneath.
- **Inside pages on narrow screens (to 56rem).** The oversized token stays at the top, with one useful thing set against it on the right, over a 4px rule: on an assessment its importance and evidence; on an edition's pages the month, followed by a line of tabs (Contents, Editorial, Shortlist); on a topic the eight row letters, the current one boxed. The list of facts follows the text. Charter, Method and Corrections get the line of tabs. The text keeps its 43rem measure on a tablet.
- **Archive on a phone:** two plates to a line.
- **Assessment side column:** Importance, Evidence (with "Self-reported by developer" or "Peer-reviewed" beneath when that applies; "Independent" is not printed), Contribution, Evidence type, Read (only when partial), Review, Cite as. Conflicts are a note under the text.
- **Links on an assessment:** only "Read the original" sits under the standfirst, joined by a jump to "Later developments" on the few pages that have them. Every other link appears lower down under a heading that says what it is: More from the authors, Responses and critiques (dated), Background.
- **No reproduced papers.** An assessment links to the work; it shows the authors' abstract only when that is in a public metadata record.
- **Charts:** one black 2px line on white, light gridlines, the latest value labeled, a crosshair on hover, and a data table under every chart.

## Avoid

Warm cream or beige paper, editorial serif body text over tinted backgrounds, generated illustrations, card grids, glassmorphism, gradient text, dark mode with neon accents, hero-then-scrolling-sections home pages, hero-metric templates, decorative sparklines, monospace as shorthand for "technical", and explanatory microcopy.
