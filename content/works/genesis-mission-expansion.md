The Genesis Mission was created by executive order in November 2025 and began as a Department of Energy program for applying AI to science. The July 22 announcement extends it across the federal government: "More than 15 Federal agencies will contribute research awards, funding opportunities, specialized scientific datasets, and research facilities to the National Science and Technology Challenges". The challenges it names range from pediatric cancer and grid planning to autonomous laboratories and the design of weapons components. The release describes the mission as "aiming to double America's R&D output".

On the same day the Department of Energy announced its first selections: "278 awards: 87 led by DOE and National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) National Laboratories, 168 led by universities, 19 led by companies, and 4 led by nonprofit organizations". The largest is a three-year, $60 million project on nuclear energy. The department notes that "Selection for award negotiations is not a commitment by DOE to issue an award or provide funding".

The White House release gives no breakdown of the $5 billion and does not say how much is additional to existing budgets. Eos reported that the White House science report issued the day before "did not mention recent and proposed cuts to science agency budgets", and that Democrats at a House hearing on July 22 pointed to grants canceled or delayed by the administration.
