The White House issued this fact sheet at a summit on October 8. It gathers announcements from several agencies under four headings and puts the total at "over $6 billion in investments across the Federal Government, industry, academia, and philanthropy". The items that carry dollar figures add up to about that amount, and most of the money is not federal.

The largest item is "$2.4B in SI for science tools and compute credits for the Genesis Mission Consortium from eleven industry partners". The pledges are $1 billion from NVIDIA, $500 million from AMD, $200 million from OpenAI, $150 million each from Anthropic and Google, $100 million each from AMP and Emerald AI, and $50 million each from AWS, Armada, Crusoe and Micron. The credits are meant to "support over 15 Federal agencies working towards National Science & Technology Challenges", the program whose federal funding was announced in July ({{genesis-mission-expansion}}). The fact sheet does not say over what period the credits run, how they are valued, who allocates them, or how a researcher outside the agencies would obtain any.

The other items with figures are:

- a $1 billion investment in scientific computing and training by universities, industry and the State of Georgia;
- more than $100 million from NSF and DOE for AI-enabled instruments and autonomous laboratories;
- a $100 million Genesis Mission Fellowship for four-year PhDs that pair AI with another discipline;
- a $215 million DOE competition to demonstrate fault-tolerant quantum computers;
- two NSF prize competitions of $33 million each;
- a $1.8 billion initiative of NIH, DOE and Biohub to build data and models of cells;
- more than $310 million in industry and philanthropic commitments to a consortium that supplies instruments and space to X-Labs, the independent research teams NSF began funding in May.

Four agencies also announced metascience programs, which the document describes as "applying the scientific method to evaluate how we fund and conduct science", and NSF agreed to cooperate with the United Kingdom's Metascience Unit on measuring research impact and improving review.

The fact sheet writes "super intelligence (SI)" throughout, where the July release linked at the foot of the page is titled as a mission on "AI for Science". Its first section begins: "Super intelligence (SI) will be the greatest accelerant of scientific progress in human history".
