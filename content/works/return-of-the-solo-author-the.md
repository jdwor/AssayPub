The average number of authors on a scientific paper has risen for decades. Akira Matsui of Kobe University looks at the other end of the distribution, the share of papers with one author, on the reasoning that if language models can do work that coauthors used to do, the effect should show first in papers written alone. The data are the full OpenAlex database, more than 300 million works of which 126 million are peer-reviewed papers, in 26 fields from 1990 to 2025.

The main finding is that "the decades-long decline in solo authorship halted and partially reversed with ChatGPT’s public release in late 2022". Fitting separate trends before and after November 2022, the change in slope is positive in 23 of 26 fields for peer-reviewed papers and significant in 22. It is largest in engineering, business, mathematics, decision sciences, psychology and computer science, and absent in chemistry and in physics and astronomy, where research depends on laboratories and instruments. The rebound is strongest for preprints.

Holding the mix of authors fixed by field, career age, citations and productivity, the probability that an active author publishes a solo paper in a given year stops falling at 2022, and the break is at least as strong among authors who had never published alone. In an embedding of paper abstracts, solo papers after 2022 shift toward computational topics relative to team papers, and an author's solo papers span a narrower range than the same kind of author's coauthored papers while staying close to that author's earlier work. Matsui reads this as researchers doing alone a slice of what they used to do with others.

Matsui writes that "Solo-authored papers remain a small minority of scientific output, and nothing in our results suggests that solo work is displacing teams as the dominant mode of production." The evidence is correlational, and the paper says so: "We therefore cannot claim that the reversal is the causal effect of LLMs; other changes at the same time, such as the fading of pandemic-era publishing, may contribute."

Within journals observed continuously from 2018 to 2024, the solo share stops short of rising: "within these venues the decline decelerates sharply rather than turns positive". In engineering, the field with the largest break, the author-level probability does not move, "so the aggregate reversal there operates through composition rather than through within-author switching". The paper does not assess the quality of the solo papers, and says of its venue filters that "they cannot rule out mill-style output within indexed venues". OpenAlex changed its ingestion and author-disambiguation methods during the period studied.
