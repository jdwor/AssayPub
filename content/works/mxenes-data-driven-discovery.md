MXenes are a family of layered carbides and nitrides. The authors searched two crystal-structure databases, the Materials Project and the Open Quantum Materials Database, for three-element compounds with the right proportions and found 541. A random-forest classifier trained on 1,050 labeled structures scored 270 of them as likely to have the layered MXene structure. Of those, 33 were tagged in the databases as having been made, and a literature search added 17. Twelve of the 50 were already known as MXenes. The preprint counts the other 38, most of them first made decades ago, as unrecognized members of the family. The journal abstract reports that "Screening reveals 33 previously synthesized but unrecognized MXene candidates".

The group then "synthesized seven multilayer MXenes" (five in the preprint) by a combustion reaction that finishes in about five minutes, and "realized 11 previously unexplored rare earth-based MXenes". The compounds were characterized by X-ray diffraction, electron microscopy, and optical and Raman spectroscopy. Their magnetic states were calculated and were not measured.

The machine-learning step is a conventional classifier used to sort database entries. The compounds were made as layered powders, and the preprint does not report separating them into single sheets. It gives phase purities from about 54% to 90%. One of the new compounds, Tm2CF2, changes color and swells in air, which the authors attribute to oxidation or hydrolysis.
