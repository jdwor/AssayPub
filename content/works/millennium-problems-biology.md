The list takes its form from the Millennium Prize Problems in mathematics. Its selection rule comes from the difficulty of judging a claim: problems were chosen to be, as the about page puts it, "very difficult to solve, but easy to validate in a simple laboratory environment", which is why aims such as solving aging are excluded: they are hard and have no agreed test. Its authors describe the set as the last reasonable eval for AI in biology. Twelve problems were published on September 19, 2026, revised two days later, and are maintained as a living list.

The molecular machines asked for do not exist in nature and would change what can be measured. A reverse translatase would read an untagged polypeptide and write a nucleic acid encoding its sequence, without a template, a barcode or a lookup, and the criterion is set in sequencing terms: at least 100 preregistered random peptides of 50 or more residues, pooled, read back at 90% accuracy with average read length of at least 25 residues. A protein amplification chain reaction would copy peptides exponentially from amino-acid monomers, with routes through nucleic acid intermediates explicitly barred as duplicating the reverse translatase problem. A complete set of polymerases running 3′ to 5′ would need processivity and error rates comparable to Taq, T7 RNA polymerase, M-MLV reverse transcriptase and Phi6 RDRP. A nitrogenase with no sequence or structural homology to the natural family would have to fix nitrogen at rates within an order of magnitude of natural enzymes while falling well below similarity thresholds to every known nitrogenase.

Other problems ask for engineering in cells and animals: a living cell whose every coding sequence, including the translation machinery, reads in quadruplet codons with no detectable triplet decoding; limb regeneration in adult wild-type mice, judged by motor and sensory tests and by whether blinded observers can tell which limb was regrown; and whole-body cryopreservation and recovery of adult mice with better than 99% viability and no permanent organ damage. Production and delivery also appear: infectious, replication-incompetent AAV and lentivirus made in bacteria at capsid-to-genome and infectivity ratios comparable to mammalian cell culture, with AAV alone counting as a partial success; and protein binders that engage preregistered intracellular targets from outside the cell at pharmacologically plausible concentrations, with transfection, electroporation and membrane disruption ruled out.

Improving Rubisco is specified against two named natural enzymes: specificity for carbon dioxide at least as high as the Galdieria partita enzyme and turnover at least as high as maize, measured in paired assays with both as controls. Programmable proteases asks for a protease designed prospectively against a blinded, accessible site in an endogenous folded protein, cleaving it efficiently in living cells with off-target cleavage comparable to proteases already in use, against 20 preregistered sites at better than 80% success. The design must arrive within 24 hours of the sites being disclosed, because "no wet lab work is allowed prior to evaluation except for the purpose of producing the designed proteins for assay". That clause rules out the step that July's reprogrammed proteases depended on: {{ai-redesigned-protease-evolution}} reached its specificity through weeks of continuous evolution against the target it was given. The weaker form of the same problem, cleaving preregistered peptide sequences in solution, is marked a partial success.

The criteria are written to close off the ways a claim escapes judgment. Preregistration fixes the target set before the attempt; blinding removes the choice of an easy site; the ban on laboratory work between disclosure and design separates prediction from screening; partial successes are defined in advance so that a weaker result is reported as what it is. The origins-of-life problem states the default for an ambiguous answer: "Solutions in which the existence of heritable genetic information is ambiguous or controversial will be rejected by default." Nothing here is a result, and the list's authors have an interest in the examination they are setting. The tests they specify would produce outcomes that are not in dispute.
