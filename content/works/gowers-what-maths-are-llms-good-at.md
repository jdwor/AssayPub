Gowers, a Fields medalist, wrote this post a few days after OpenAI announced solutions to ten open problems, among them the first construction of a non-sofic group. The post observes that "it is notable that the most famous problems they have solved have almost all been with counterexamples rather than proofs."

Gowers doubts that the models have a special talent for counterexamples. Comparing a theorem of Vinogradov in number theory with a construction of Gluskin in Banach space theory, which have the same logical form, the post argues that whether a result counts as a counterexample depends on its mathematical context. The explanation offered is that the problems solved so far suit "wide knowledge and the ability to explore many paths of the search tree that humans would judge to have a low probability of success." The post lists eight ways mathematicians look for examples and suggests that current models are suited to those that rely on standard constructions, and less to those that need a sense of which direction is promising.

From use of ChatGPT on open problems, Gowers reports being offered approaches that "sound promising until I think about them carefully". The post proposes testing the idea with weaker models that have been kept from the mathematical literature.

Gowers adds: "I am not making any assertions of the form “LLMs will never be able to do X”." Gowers would consider the limitation overcome "if an LLM were to come up with a proof that was as surprising to me as the solution of the cap-set problem was in 2016".

The post is an argument from experience, and Gowers does not "pretend to have a scientific answer".
