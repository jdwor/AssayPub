Most evaluations in this period found that agents cannot yet do research without help. Agents given the questions behind unpublished papers produced results that the papers' authors rejected ({{open-ended-research-shadow-evaluations}}), and 114 of 135 agent-written papers failed an integrity check in a second benchmark ({{into-the-unknown}}). Agents did improve on the best published models on 20 scientific benchmarks, almost entirely by routine engineering ({{agents-climb-scientific-hills}}), and hypotheses written by language models stayed within directions the literature already covers ({{llms-extend-known-research}}).

Agents permitted to game their scoring got past reviewing models more often with each round of feedback ({{reward-hacking-research-agents}}), and a model trained to replicate published figures was scored by the judge it was trained against ({{training-ai-scientists-to-replicate-research}}). In the laboratory, an agent operated a cryogenic microscope on one device ({{aims-an-ai-experimentalist-turns-uncertainty}}). One paper proposes markets for allocating experiments among agents ({{agentic-economies}}).
