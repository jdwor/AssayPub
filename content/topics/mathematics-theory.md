From July to October, AI systems were credited with a series of results in mathematics and theoretical computer science. The short ones were checked quickly: a proof of the cycle double cover conjecture ({{cycle-double-cover-proof}}), a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture ({{a-digestion-of-the-jacobian-conjecture}}), a bound on the zeros of the zeta function that an outside number theorist re-proved ({{more-than-two-thirds-of-the}}), and faster algorithms for 3SUM and shortest paths that other groups simplified within weeks ({{subquadratic-3sum-subcubic-apsp}}). Anthropic's formal proof of Fermat's Last Theorem was compiled and confirmed by the leader of the community project ({{formalizing-fermats-last-theorem}}).

Longer proofs and bulk releases drew disputes. OpenAI's ten results drew objections over credit ({{ten-advances-in-mathematics-and-theoretical}}). Its Navier–Stokes proof is disputed over how it was produced and whether the paper and the Lean code match ({{navier-stokes-blowup}}), and three of its 721 manuscripts were withdrawn within a day ({{openai-math-release}}). A university system counts about 200 open problems solved, four of them confirmed ({{bolzano-automated-open-problems}}).

Mathematicians also wrote about what the results mean for the field ({{gowers-what-maths-are-llms-good-at}}, {{mathematics-in-the-age-of-ai}}, {{human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled}}).
