Everything assessed in the period is preclinical, and most of the numbers come from the developers. Anthropic's account of six drug-development partnerships rests on each partner's figures, and its technical report gives a pilot in seven mice at P = 0.057 by litter ({{anthropic-drug-development}}). Nine replayed campaigns met their objectives wherever the property models were accurate on the chemistry proposed, and no compound was synthesized ({{magi-industrial-campaigns}}).

In chemistry the test is faster: a model confined to an explicit reaction network named three variables controlling selectivity in carbon dioxide reduction, and a copper-iron oxide made after the predictions were fixed tripled acetate selectivity ({{cothinker-catalyst-selectivity}}). A peptide-interface structure predictor recovered about half of an 88-complex benchmark; no designed molecule was made ({{vilya-2-molecular-interfaces}}).

The period's main review found evidence of clinically relevant impact still limited ({{artificial-intelligence-in-drug-discovery-what}}), and Derek Lowe reads it as putting the gap at Phase II success, the stage least touched by current methods ({{lowe-ai-drug-discovery-really}}). Ruxandra Teslo places the constraint in trials, data access and patent incentives ({{intelligence-is-not-the-main-bottleneck}}).
