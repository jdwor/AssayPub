The United States extended the Genesis Mission across more than 15 federal agencies in July ({{genesis-mission-expansion}}) and in October announced $2.4 billion in tools and computing credits pledged to it by eleven companies ({{golden-age-summit-fact-sheet}}). Neither announcement gives terms or says how much of the money is new. At a summit in Dublin the European Commission launched a scheme under which companies and philanthropies pledge model access, computing and other resources to researchers ({{commission-launches-raise-pledging-initiative-to}}).

In mathematics, institutions responded to the companies' releases. A group of nine mathematicians published guidelines for releasing AI-generated mathematics ({{responsible-release-of-ai-generated-mathematics}}), and arXiv limited submissions per submitter ({{fair-moderation-equitable-access-and-ai}}). Daniel Litt describes how present incentives could waste what the tools make possible ({{the-end-of-mathematics}}), and an essay by Google DeepMind staff argues for funding experimental facilities ({{conjecture-machines}}).
