Assay is a monthly review of open work in AI for science. It does not publish original research. Each month it scans preprint servers, laboratory and company websites, working-paper series, newsletters, code and data repositories and funders' announcements. It selects a small number of items and publishes an assessment of each.

## Background

Scientific journals have historically registered priority, certified results, distributed and archived papers, and directed readers' attention. Preprint servers and the web now handle registration, distribution and archiving. Certification and filtering are still done mainly by journals, through peer review that takes months and applies only to papers.

In AI for science, much of the significant work never goes through that process. It appears as preprints, technical reports on company websites, datasets, benchmarks, software and essays. Reports from AI companies are widely read and rarely reviewed independently, and the companies have a commercial interest in the results.

AI tools have also increased the volume of research output. Monthly arXiv submissions reached a record in September 2026. The same tools make some kinds of checking faster, such as tracing a quoted figure to its source or comparing a claim with the text that supports it. Assay uses them for that purpose, with a human editor responsible for what is published.

## Process

Software collects new items from the sources listed on the [method](../method/) page. An AI model sorts them into eight subject areas, puts up to ten works in each on the edition's plate, and drafts assessments of the items it selects. An editor reviews the draft against the sources and signs the edition. Each assessment states whether an editor has reviewed it.

Each assessment describes what the work claims, rates its importance and the strength of its evidence in fixed terms, and records what was checked and any conflicts of interest. Assay links to each work and does not reproduce it.

Each edition also lists the works that were shortlisted and not selected.

## Policies

Criticism of a work is published only if it identifies a specific passage or claim, can be verified from the source, and affects a main conclusion. Authors may respond, and responses are published with the assessment.

Errors in assessments are corrected and listed on the [corrections](../corrections/) page.

The model that drafts assessments is Claude, made by Anthropic, which also publishes research in this field. Assessments of work by Anthropic or its competitors say so.

Assay does not accept payment for coverage.

## Status

Assay is a pilot, and no edition has yet been reviewed by an editor.
